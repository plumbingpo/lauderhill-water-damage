Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Lauderhill Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Lauderhill, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

Hazard Check and Containment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
