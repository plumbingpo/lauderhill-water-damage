Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Lauderhill

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

On every Lauderhill job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Cabinet Damage

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.
