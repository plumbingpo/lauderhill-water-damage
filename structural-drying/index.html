Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Lauderhill Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Lauderhill

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.
